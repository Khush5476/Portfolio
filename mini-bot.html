Robotics Competition

MiniBot

I have made 2 iterations of a 6x6x6 inch competition robot optimized to stack and flip glowing cubes in Cubic Chaos, a quantum-themed arena game.

The Game

Cubic Chaos

The Challenge

Cubic Chaos is set in a futuristic quantum arena where robots must manipulate glowing cubes to score points. Each robot must fit inside a 6x6x6 inch cube at the start of a match, and teams earn points by flipping cubes to their color and stacking them in the endgame for bonus value.

Across three competitions, I redesigned and rebuilt my robot by learning what worked, what did not, and how to improve it each time.

Robot Design 1

Stacking

First iteration focused on flipping cubes while also storing them so opponents could not flip them back. I designed it so it could carry four cubes in a square formation, and in the endgame the servo system would allow stacked cubes to count as two stacks of two cubes.

Reflection

What I Learned Across This Season

01

Prioritizing a faster flip mechanism proved more valuable than over-optimizing for stacking, because consistent scoring depended on quick, reliable cube manipulation.

02

Reducing the size of the flipper made the robot easier to control, improving stability and making the mechanism more precise under pressure.

03

Adding more wheels increased traction and torque, which made the robot stronger in matchups against other competitors and more stable during aggressive drives.

04

Making design changes and testing new ideas taught me more than stubbornly sticking to one concept, showing that iteration is one of the fastest ways to improve.
