Robotics Project

Robotic Hand

A 3D-printed robotic hand designed to replicate natural finger movement using servos, string, rubber bands, and hand-tracking software.

01 Design & Fabrication

Hand and Fingers

I started by designing the hand around my own measurements so the final model would have realistic proportions and be roughly the same size as my hand. This gave me a physical reference to work from instead of designing the hand around arbitrary dimensions.

For the fingers, I created cylinders and then sliced both sides at a 45-degree angle. This created the individual bending sections needed for the fingers to move naturally.

I then added internal holes to control how each finger moved. The front section had a hole for the string to pass through. When the string was pulled by the servo, it would bend the finger.

Behind this, I added two more holes for a rubber band. The rubber band kept the finger upright and allowed it to return to its home position when the string was loosened.

Finally, I created an opening for the thumb. Because the thumb needs more movement than the other fingers, I used two servos: one servo controls the thumb moving left and right, while the second servo controls the thumb curling motion.

Design Focus Realistic hand proportions + mechanical finger movement
02 Mechanical Assembly

Arm With Servos

Once the hand was created, I started working on the arm that would hold all of the servos and connect the mechanical system together.

I began by creating a large cylindrical base. This gave the arm a wider footprint, making the overall build more stable when the servos were moving the fingers.

I also added two cylindrical pieces that protruded from the hand. To connect the hand and arm, I designed two matching indents in the arm. This allowed the two pieces to connect using a press-fit assembly.

After the main structure was complete, I created dedicated mounting areas for all of the servos. This kept the motors positioned correctly while allowing them to pull the strings controlling each finger.

Finally, I added a hole at the back of the arm so the servo wires could pass through the structure without interfering with the moving parts.

Base Wide cylindrical support
Connection Press-fit hand mount
Servos Dedicated mounting areas
Wiring Rear wire passage
03 Electronics & Software

Hardware

At first, I wanted to fit all of the wiring inside the physical build. However, I did not have a board that could connect all of the servos directly, so I used a breadboard to give myself more space for connecting the wires.

The wiring ended up being somewhat messy, but the breadboard gave me enough room to connect everything and get the system working reliably.

For the software side, I downloaded the MediaPipe library and created code that could detect my hand and track its movement. I then mapped each servo to its corresponding finger.

This meant that moving one of my fingers would correspond to movement from the matching servo on the robotic hand. After connecting all of the wires and debugging the system, the robotic hand was able to respond to my hand movements.

Control System MediaPipe hand tracking → finger movement → servo control
04 Final Build

Final Design

The final design combines the mechanical structure, servo system, string mechanisms, rubber bands, and hand-tracking software into one working robotic hand.

The design was built around the goal of making the fingers move in a way that corresponds to my own hand movements. Each finger has its own mechanical system, while the thumb uses two servos to provide additional movement.

One of the biggest parts of the build was getting the mechanical and electronic systems to work together. The hand had to be designed around the movement of the strings and rubber bands, while the arm had to provide enough space for the servos and wiring.

After assembling the parts, connecting the electronics, and debugging the hand-tracking system, I was able to create a functional robotic hand that responds to my hand movements.

Reflection

What I Learned From the Build

01

Designing around real measurements made the robotic hand feel more realistic and gave me a better understanding of designing parts around a physical reference.

02

Mechanical systems need to be designed around how the parts will actually move, not just how they look in CAD.

03

Working with servos, strings, rubber bands, and 3D-printed parts taught me how mechanical and electronic systems can work together.

04

Debugging the wiring and MediaPipe tracking showed me how important testing and iteration are when combining hardware and software.
